--- title: "Weekly News Digest: June 28 – July 5, 2026" range: "June 28 – July 5, 2026" date: 2026-07-06 visitors: "0001337" ---

America marked its 250th birthday under a storm-delayed, politically charged "Salute to America," while overseas Russia's July 2 assault on Kyiv killed at least 31 and Venezuela's earthquake toll passed 2,600. Anthropic shipped Claude Sonnet 5, a Swedish court hit Google with a record $1.5B judgment, and Taylor Swift married Travis Kelce at Madison Square Garden.

★ US Politics & Policy

America's 250th and Trump's "Salute to America"

The July 4 semiquincentennial was the week's defining domestic event. After severe weather forced a roughly two-hour evacuation of the National Mall, President Trump delivered a Salute to America speech that veered from patriotic tribute into campaign themes — praising the Iran war, likening communism to "cancer," and vowing "no mail-in ballots, except for illness, disability, military deployment, or travel" while pledging to pass the SAVE America Act. He had delivered similar remarks from Mount Rushmore on July 3. A masked Patriot Front contingent also marched in Washington during the celebrations.

NPR, PBS NewsHour, Axios, Newsweek, The National

Supreme Court ends TPS for Haiti and Syria

In a 6-3 decision in Mullin v. Doe (decided June 25), the Court allowed the administration to terminate Temporary Protected Status for Haiti and Syria, ruling the TPS statute bars judicial review of most termination determinations. Justice Alito wrote for the majority; Justice Kagan dissented. The ruling affects roughly 350,000 Haitians and about 6,000 Syrians; USCIS set TPS work-authorization expiry for July 10, 2026.

SCOTUSblog, NPR, NBC News, American Immigration Council

SAVE America Act paralyzes the House

For the fourth time, the GOP's signature election bill derailed the House agenda. On June 30, 14 Republicans tanked a party-line procedural vote meant to advance the must-pass NDAA, which Speaker Mike Johnson had tried to package with SAVE. Leaders canceled votes and sent members into an early July 4 recess; the House returns July 13. The bill lacks the 60 Senate votes needed.

Axios, CBS News, The Washington Post

★ World News

Russia's deadly July 2 assault on Kyiv

Russia launched more than 70 missiles (28 ballistic — a single-attack record for the capital) and roughly 500 drones overnight into July 2. The toll rose to 31 dead and over 100 injured, the third-deadliest attack on Kyiv of the war. Moscow called it retaliation for Ukrainian strikes on its oil refineries; Zelensky pressed Trump to accelerate Patriot interceptor deliveries.

NPR, CNN, The Kyiv Independent, Time

Venezuela earthquake aftermath

The June 24 twin earthquakes (magnitude 7.2 and 7.5, 39 seconds apart) — Venezuela's strongest in over a century — dominated South American news. By July 3, Al Jazeera cited 2,645 confirmed dead with as many as 38,500 missing. International rescue teams, including US teams from Fairfax County and Los Angeles County, worked the rubble; a 3-year-old was pulled alive after six days.

Al Jazeera, NPR, UN News

US-Iran war aftermath

Following the February 28 strikes that killed Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and the June 17 Islamabad Memorandum ending the war, funeral ceremonies ran July 4-9 in Tehran, drawing an expected 20 million-plus mourners. Diplomacy over the Strait of Hormuz continued in Doha, with VP Vance saying talks were "going well"; strait traffic remained far below normal.

Al Jazeera, CNN, Britannica, House of Commons Library

Vatican schism

On July 2, the Vatican declared the traditionalist Society of St. Pius X in schism and excommunicated its bishops and priests after it consecrated four bishops without the pope's consent.

NPR, Wikipedia Current Events

★ Tech News

Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 5 (June 30)

Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5, its "most agentic Sonnet yet," as the default model for Claude Free and Pro plans. It posts 63.2% on SWE-Bench Pro agentic coding (up from Sonnet 4.6's 58.1%, approaching Opus 4.8's 69.2%) at introductory API pricing of $2/$10 per million input/output tokens through August 31, 2026.

Anthropic, TechCrunch, MacRumors

OpenAI previews GPT-5.6 Sol (June 26)

OpenAI previewed the GPT-5.6 series (Sol/Terra/Luna), featuring an "ultra" mode that splits work across subagents. Access was gated to about 20 trusted partners via API and Codex at the US government's request, following a June 2 executive order on frontier-model cyber capabilities.

OpenAI, TechCrunch, Axios, VentureBeat

Google hit with record Swedish antitrust judgment (July 1)

Stockholm's Patent and Market Court ordered Alphabet's Google to pay ~$1.5 billion to Klarna-owned PriceRunner, finding Google had for years unlawfully favored its own price-comparison service. Presiding judge Linda Kullberg called it the largest ever decided in a Swedish competition case. Google plans to appeal.

Reuters, Claims Journal, Tech Times

Dev tooling

Deno 2.9 (GA June 25, patch v2.9.1 July 1) introduced deno desktop for building native cross-platform desktop apps, positioned as an Electron/Tauri alternative. PHP 8.5.8, a maintenance release, shipped July 2 with security fixes.

Deno blog, InfoWorld, PHP.Watch

★ Arkansas

Record $655M surplus

Arkansas ended FY2026 (June 30) with a $655 million general-revenue surplus — the fifth-largest in state history and nearly $300 million more than the prior year, pushing reserves to $4 billion. Gov. Sarah Huckabee Sanders said it proves "conservative leadership works" as she pursues phasing out the income tax; Senate Minority Leader Greg Leding criticized budgeting toward surpluses amid high food insecurity.

Arkansas Advocate, Talk Business & Politics, KATV

Direct-democracy amendment qualifies

On July 3, Protect AR Rights submitted 108,837 signatures (above the 90,704 required) for the Arkansas Ballot Measure Rights Amendment, days after a federal judge ruled several petition-process restrictions unconstitutional. The measure would make it harder for the legislature to override voter-passed measures.

Arkansas Democrat-Gazette, Arkansas Times, KY3

This Week in Arkansas History

On July 4, 1819, the Arkansas Territory formally came into being, with territorial judge Andrew Horatio Scott reporting for duty at Arkansas Post. On the same date in 1863, the Battle of Helena took place: Union Maj. Gen. Benjamin Prentiss, with about 4,129 men, repelled Lt. Gen. Theophilus Holmes's roughly 7,646 Confederates (about 1,696 Confederate casualties vs. 239 Union). The Confederate defeat came the day after Gettysburg and the same day Vicksburg fell.

Magnolia Reporter / Encyclopedia of Arkansas, American Battlefield Trust

★ Pop Culture

Taylor Swift marries Travis Kelce (July 3)

The apex celebrity wedding of 2026 took place at Madison Square Garden, with actor Adam Sandler as officiant and Stevie Nicks performing. Some 1,000 guests attended — including Selena Gomez, Gigi Hadid, Bradley Cooper, Steven Spielberg, Tom Brady, and Ed Sheeran. The couple wore Christian Dior Haute Couture.

CBS News, CNN, PBS/AP

World Cup and the Balogun controversy

The 2026 FIFA World Cup (co-hosted by US, Mexico, Canada; final July 19) reached the Round of 16. FIFA suspended US striker Folarin Balogun's red-card ban after Trump personally called FIFA president Gianni Infantino — a move UEFA said "crossed a red line." Norway (with Haaland) eliminated Brazil on July 5.

NPR, Al Jazeera, ESPN
