The labor market cracked open the week. The July jobs report landed August 7 showing the US economy unexpectedly lost 23,000 jobs, with steep downward revisions erasing much of the spring's gains — a single datapoint that reframed the entire economic and Federal Reserve policy debate and rippled through markets. Washington still delivered concrete outcomes: Todd Blanche was confirmed attorney general 50-49, a federal appeals court blocked Trump's $400M White House ballroom, Trump reopened his bid to fire Fed Governor Lisa Cook, and progressive Abdul El-Sayed won Michigan's marquee Senate primary. Abroad brought escalation — intensified Russian strikes on Ukraine and renewed Israeli attacks in Gaza — while tech saw steady consolidation and a record-shattering box office capped the week.

🏛️ US Politics & Policy

July jobs report — US economy shed 23,000 jobs; prior months slashed by 103,000

Payrolls fell 23,000 in July against a consensus expecting a gain of about 83,000. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported August 7 that the unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1% as labor-force participation slid to 61.4%, its lowest in more than five years, while May was revised down 66,000 and June down 37,000 — leaving the two months a combined 103,000 lower and the trailing 12-month average at just 34,000 jobs a month. Losses concentrated in government (–53,000) and leisure and hospitality (–40,000). The report scrambled the Fed debate, moving labor-market risk to center stage.

BLS, CNBC, NBC News, Quartz, Indeed Hiring Lab

Todd Blanche confirmed as attorney general, 50-49

The Senate confirmed Trump's former personal attorney early Saturday, August 8, in a near-party-line vote; Republicans Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski joined all Democrats in opposition. Blanche, now the 88th attorney general, had faced rare intra-GOP resistance tied to the DOJ's $1.8 billion Anti-Weaponization Fund and to concerns he could not run the department independently of the president he once defended.

Washington Post, CNN, NPR, NBC News, The Hill

Appeals court blocks Trump's $400M White House ballroom

In a 2-1 decision Friday, August 7, the DC Circuit affirmed an injunction halting above-ground construction of the 90,000-square-foot ballroom rising where the East Wing once stood, holding that only Congress can authorize such changes to the White House. Judges Patricia Millett and Bradley Garcia wrote that each president is a temporary tenant rather than the owner of the Executive Residence; Trump-appointee Neomi Rao dissented. The panel stayed its own ruling for 14 days, and Trump vowed to appeal to the Supreme Court.

NBC News, CBS News, ABC News, PBS/AP

Trump renews effort to fire Fed Governor Lisa Cook

A White House letter made public Friday, August 7, gave Cook until August 26 to respond to mortgage-fraud allegations she denies, reviving a removal effort the Supreme Court blocked 5-4 in late June on due-process grounds. Cook's attorney Abbe Lowell called the allegations baseless and vowed to fight. Trump remains the first president in the Fed's 112-year history to attempt to fire a board member, making this a live test of central-bank independence.

Washington Post, CNBC, CBS News, Fortune

Progressive Abdul El-Sayed wins Michigan Senate primary

On August 4, El-Sayed defeated establishment-backed Rep. Haley Stevens for the Democratic nomination for Michigan's open Senate seat, overcoming a nearly 60-to-1 outside-money gap: per AdImpact, outside groups spent roughly $52.3 million boosting Stevens versus about $853,000 for El-Sayed. He faces Republican Mike Rogers, who ran unopposed, in November, in a contest both parties frame as a test of the Democratic Party's direction.

Washington Post, CNN, NBC News, Bridge Michigan

Senate opens floor action on crypto CLARITY Act, punts vote to September

After a marathon overnight session, Senate leadership filed a cloture motion August 8 to begin floor consideration of the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act — the furthest the crypto market-structure bill has advanced. Majority Leader John Thune confirmed no final vote before the August recess; the Senate returns September 14. Unresolved ethics provisions continue to block the bipartisan votes needed to reach 60.

🌍 World News

Russia intensifies strikes on Ukraine ahead of Independence Day

Russian strikes near Kyiv killed four people, including a 3-year-old boy, on August 8. A subsequent missile attack on Odesa left, per President Volodymyr Zelensky, more than 300,000 families without power. Ukraine's Strategic Communications Center warned August 8, citing US intelligence, that Russia is preparing a major strike campaign on Kyiv's energy infrastructure ahead of Ukraine's August 24 Independence Day.

Al Jazeera, Kyiv Independent, Institute for the Study of War

Israel escalates Gaza attacks, resists US-backed disarmament roadmap

Despite a Trump-announced roadmap for a second ceasefire stage, Israeli strikes killed more than two dozen Palestinians over the August 1-3 weekend. PM Benjamin Netanyahu said August 4 that Israel would not withdraw beyond the "Yellow Line" until Hamas is fully disarmed, appearing to contradict the plan, while Trump's Board of Peace insisted no Israeli pullback would precede disarmament — underscoring the fragility of the post-October 2025 ceasefire.

Al Jazeera, UN News, Times of Israel

Iran signals readiness to resume war with US amid Hormuz standoff

Iranian military authorities said August 4 they are prepared to resume fighting if negotiations again collapse, emphasizing they have rebuilt and increased missile production during the truce. Washington signaled a Strait of Hormuz agreement may be imminent, while Trump renewed his demand for a deal or "total surrender." The April 8 ceasefire, mediated by Pakistan and extended indefinitely on April 21, has been strained repeatedly.

Al Jazeera, CNN

💻 Tech News

OpenAI acquires presentation startup NextSlide

OpenAI's NextSlide team is now working on ChatGPT. TechCrunch reported August 8 that OpenAI acquired the presentation startup, whose founder Ahmed Beshry described a product that turns prompts, notes, documents, or research into polished, editable presentations; the deal had actually closed earlier in the year. Terms were undisclosed. The move fits an aggressive 2026 AI-consolidation pattern.

TechCrunch

Python 3.14.7 and 3.13.15 ship

The Python Software Foundation released Python 3.14.7 on August 5 — the seventh maintenance release of the 3.14 series, with roughly 499 bugfixes from 86 contributors — alongside 3.13.15. The 3.14 line is notable for promoting free-threaded mode to a supported feature, an improved tail-call-based JIT, Zstandard compression in the stdlib, and t-strings.

Python Insider (blog.python.org)

Node.js advances to 26.7.0

The current Node.js release line advanced to 26.7.0 on August 5, per version trackers, as the runtime continues its six-month major-release cadence; the 26.x line is slated to move to Active LTS after its six months as "Current."

VersionLog, endoflife.date

Bending Spoons to acquire Airtable for ~$1.285 billion

Tech Funding News reported August 6 that Bending Spoons agreed to acquire the workflow platform Airtable in an all-cash deal valued at about $1.285 billion, continuing the Italian app-holding company's pattern of buying established software brands.

🐗 Arkansas

Cyclosporiasis outbreak balloons to 237 cases

The state now has 237 lab-confirmed cyclosporiasis cases, with 144 in Benton County. The Arkansas Department of Health's count as of Friday, August 7 was up 32 since Wednesday. The scale is extraordinary: Arkansas recorded just seven cases in all of 2025 and only 10 as of mid-July 2026. Officials also pushed back on social-media posts falsely promoting the parasitic infection as a weight-loss method.

Arkansas Democrat-Gazette, NWA Democrat-Gazette, KAIT8

Arkansas State University holds first veterinary white coat ceremony

A-State's new College of Veterinary Medicine held a white coat ceremony Saturday, August 8, at the Fowler Center for its inaugural class of 120 students, led by Dean Dr. Heidi Banse, marking the program's first milestone before classes begin August 12.

KAIT8

US citizens sue Centerton police over ICE-related detention

A mother and son who are US citizens filed a federal lawsuit — filed July 30 in the Western District of Arkansas and reported August 5 — against three Centerton police officers and Chief Cody Harper, alleging they were illegally detained at ICE's request during a March 2025 traffic stop despite showing valid identification.

Arkansas Advocate, Arkansas Times, KARK

📚 This Week in Arkansas History

On August 3, 1819, the Arkansas Territory's first provisional legislature completed its organizing session at Arkansas Post. Acting Governor Robert Crittenden's session established five counties — Arkansas, Lawrence, Pulaski, Clark and Hempstead — and divided them into two judicial circuits, laying the foundation of territorial government and its court system.

🎬 Pop Culture

Biggest box-office weekend in history

"Spider-Man: Brand New Day" opened to $360 million domestically, topping "Avengers: Endgame's" record. Over the weekend of August 1-3, Sony's film beat the 2019 mark of $357.1 million for the biggest opening weekend in domestic history. Paired with "The Odyssey," it drove the biggest overall box-office weekend ever recorded — a result Comscore said sets up a likely $4 billion summer and $10 billion year.

NBC News, Hollywood Reporter, Variety, CNBC

Theatrical rebound reshapes Hollywood

Industry data show 2026 on track for its strongest year since the pandemic, with 115-120 wide releases — roughly in line with the 120 films major studios debuted in 2019 — as legacy studios ramp volume and Amazon MGM Studios returns to theatrical with 13 releases, up from three in 2025. Executives credit "couch fatigue" and a renewed communal moviegoing appetite.

Variety, Hollywood Reporter
