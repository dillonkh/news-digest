The week ran on endings. The Supreme Court closed a blockbuster term, Britain lost a prime minister, an economic legend died at 100, and Washington and Tehran inched toward the end of a war — all while a rocket company kept rewiring the AI industry and Woody and Buzz shattered a box-office record. A dense seven days with consequences that will outlast the news cycle.

🏛️ US Politics & Policy

Supreme Court closes its term with landmark rulings

The justices wrapped a consequential term on June 30, but the final-week sprint delivered the fireworks. On June 30 the Court rejected President Trump's bid to end birthright citizenship, with Chief Justice Roberts writing for the majority that the 14th Amendment guarantees it to virtually all children born on US soil. The same day it upheld state bans on transgender girls in publicly funded school sports, and it lifted a decades-old cap on party spending coordinated with candidates.

NPR, U.S. News & World Report, Roll Call, CNN

A 6-3 Court expands presidential firing power

On June 29 the Court overturned a 91-year-old precedent, ruling that Trump lawfully fired FTC Commissioner Rebecca Slaughter without cause and broadening a president's control over independent agencies. In a narrower split it allowed the Federal Reserve's Lisa Cook to keep her post for now, and it held that states may count mail-in ballots postmarked by Election Day but arriving later — a rebuff of Trump's attacks on mail voting.

NPR, CNN, SCOTUSblog

🌍 World News

Keir Starmer resigns as UK prime minister

On June 22, an emotional Starmer announced outside Downing Street that he would step down, having lost the confidence of his parliamentary party. Less than two years after a landslide 2024 win, he was undone by disastrous May local elections and the rise of Reform UK. Former Greater Manchester mayor Andy Burnham, freshly returned to Parliament via a by-election, is the overwhelming favorite to succeed him, with a leadership timetable running into July. Britain is set for its seventh prime minister in a decade.

CNN, Al Jazeera, ABC News, Vatican News

US waives Iran oil sanctions as talks advance

On June 22 the US Treasury issued a 60-day waiver on Iranian oil sanctions after VP JD Vance said Tehran would allow IAEA nuclear inspectors to return, following talks in Switzerland. The move flows from a June 17 memorandum of understanding aimed at a lasting settlement after roughly 40 days of fighting. The two sides still offered clashing accounts of what was actually agreed, and Iran maintained that formal nuclear negotiations had not begun.

Al Jazeera, PBS NewsHour, The New Arab, House of Commons Library

💻 Tech News

SpaceX's $60B Cursor deal reshapes the AI coding market

The consequences of SpaceX's June 16 agreement to buy Cursor-maker Anysphere for $60 billion in stock rippled through the week. Described as the largest venture-backed acquisition on record, the all-stock deal folds the leading AI coding tool into Elon Musk's xAI/SpaceX empire and is expected to close in Q3, pending antitrust review. It sets up an unusual dynamic with Anthropic — a product-layer rival whose training also runs on SpaceX infrastructure.

TechCrunch, CNBC, Forbes, The Numbers

Musk says Grok 4.5 enters private beta

On June 28, Musk announced on X that Grok 4.5 — built on a 1.5-trillion-parameter foundation model and supplemented with Cursor coding data — had entered private beta at SpaceX and Tesla, claiming early evaluations near or exceeding Anthropic's Opus. The claims are unverified vendor benchmarks; independent evaluation hasn't happened yet.

ExplainX, CryptoBriefing, Build Fast with AI

🐗 Arkansas

AG Griffin sues Roblox and Discord over child safety

On June 22, Arkansas Attorney General Tim Griffin announced lawsuits against gaming platform Roblox and messaging service Discord, alleging the platforms expose children to sexual predators. The filings extend Griffin's pattern of targeting tech platforms over child-safety and design concerns. Separately, strong storms swept eastward across central Arkansas that same morning, bringing flash-flooding risk to the northern and central parts of the state.

Arkansas Democrat-Gazette (NWA), THV11

🎬 Pop Culture

Toy Story 5 shatters franchise and 2026 records

Pixar's Toy Story 5 opened June 19 to $159.7 million domestically — the biggest debut of 2026 and the best opening in franchise and Pixar history. Its $310 million global launch topped Inside Out 2 and drove the first $200 million domestic three-day weekend of the year. It held the top spot the following weekend (June 26-28), with Warner Bros.' Supergirl debuting to a softer $38 million.

Variety, The Hollywood Reporter, Deadline, Rotten Tomatoes, Wikipedia

Alan Greenspan dies at 100

Former Federal Reserve chair Alan Greenspan died June 22 at his Washington home from complications of Parkinson's disease. The "Maestro" who led the Fed under four presidents from 1987 to 2006 was lionized for the long 1990s boom and later faulted for the deregulation that critics tie to the 2008 financial crisis. He was married to NBC News correspondent Andrea Mitchell.

NBC News, NPR, CNBC, Al Jazeera, The Wall Street Journal
