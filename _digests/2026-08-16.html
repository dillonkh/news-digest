The week was dominated by the ongoing US–Iran war and its economic fallout, as the Strait of Hormuz stayed largely blocked and President Trump claimed "total control" of the waterway. At home, the National Guard deployment to Washington hit its one-year mark and was formalized to run through 2029, landing the same week a tame July inflation report gave the Federal Reserve cover to hold rates. Abroad, a magnitude-7.4 earthquake devastated Colombia's coffee region and Europe saw its first total solar eclipse in decades.

🏛️ US Politics & Policy

National Guard deployment in DC hits one year, extended through 2029

August marked one year since troops were deployed to Washington, with roughly 4,636 still on the streets and the Pentagon confirming a plan to keep them through the end of Trump's term in January 2029. An estimate provided to Sen. Elizabeth Warren, citing a DOD figure dated August 4 covering FY2027–2029, put the added cost at about $1.4 billion. DC's own data showed violent crime — already near multi-decade lows before the deployment — up about 3% on the year.

Washington Post, NPR, Associated Press, Al Jazeera, The Week

July inflation comes in tame at 3.4%

The Consumer Price Index rose 0.1% in July and 3.4% year-over-year, down from 3.5% in June, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported August 12. Core CPI rose 0.2% on the month and 2.5% annually, matching forecasts. Combined with a lackluster July jobs report, the reading was widely read as giving the Fed cover to hold rates at its September meeting — suggesting war-driven oil pressure had not yet decisively reached consumer prices.

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Kiplinger, Investing.com

Congress passes stopgap to avert a shutdown

Congress cleared the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act (H.R. 6500) to fund the government past a late-September deadline, with the Senate acting in an overnight session before its August recess. It was among the week's few concrete legislative outcomes.

U.S. Senate roll-call records, Cascadia Advocate

Senate voter-ID measure fails

A cloture motion to proceed to S. 5271, which would require photo identification under the Help America Vote Act, failed 52–46 at the edge of the window. The vote fell short of the 60 needed to advance.

Congress Vote Tracker, senate.gov

DOJ revives denaturalization as an enforcement tool

The Justice Department deployed a rarely used mechanism to strip naturalized Americans of citizenship, drawing national scrutiny during the week. Critics framed it as an aggressive expansion of executive immigration enforcement.

PBS NewsHour

Alabama holds redrawn congressional primaries

Alabama held special primaries August 11 in four districts redrawn after the Supreme Court narrowed Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act. The contests were part of a broader 2026 redistricting churn in which courts had allowed both Texas's GOP-friendly map and California's Democratic-friendly Proposition 50 map to stand, roughly canceling each other out for the midterms.

CBS News, SCOTUSblog

🌍 World News

US–Iran war grinds on over the Strait of Hormuz

Control of the Strait of Hormuz dominated the nearly six-month-old war, with Trump claiming August 12–13 that the US had "total control" and floating a plan to declare the strait US territory. Iran disputed the claim, and UK Maritime Trade Operations reported traffic at about 17% of the pre-war average. Iran and Oman signaled they were nearing a deal on managing the waterway, though Tehran tied a full reopening to US concessions.

CNN, CBS News, Congressional Research Service

Magnitude-7.4 earthquake devastates Colombia

A powerful quake struck western Colombia on August 10, hitting the coffee-region cities of Pereira and Cali hardest — the country's strongest in a century. By August 13, President Abelardo de la Espriella reported 281 dead, 3,971 injured and 379 missing, and declared a national disaster.

Associated Press, NPR, CNN, CNBC, Al Jazeera

Europe sees its first total solar eclipse in decades

A total solar eclipse crossed the Arctic, Iceland and northern Spain on August 12 — mainland Europe's first since 1999 and Spain's first since 1905. Millions gathered along the path; Spanish authorities ran extensive eye-safety campaigns and reported no casualties.

NASA, NPR, PBS NewsHour, CNN, Euronews

Lebanon abolishes the death penalty

Lebanon's parliament voted to abolish capital punishment, a first for the Middle East. Elsewhere, Ukraine struck Russia's Black Sea port of Novorossiysk despite US warnings, and former Chinese premier Zhu Rongji died at 97.

Morning Star, AnewZ

💻 Tech News

Google releases Gemini 3.7 Flash

Google shipped Gemini 3.7 Flash on August 13, billing it as its "most intelligent workhorse model" for coding and agents just 23 days after 3.6 Flash. It posted sizable coding-benchmark gains, kept a roughly 1M-token context window, and launched at introductory pricing of $0.75/$3.75 per million input/output tokens through year-end. Coverage stressed the flagship Gemini 3.5 Pro remained delayed, framing Google as still chasing Anthropic and OpenAI at the frontier.

Google, Bloomberg, Axios, 9to5Google

Databricks raises $5B at a $190B valuation

The data-and-AI platform closed a $5 billion strategic round led by Coatue on August 13, a 42% jump from its $134 billion valuation in February. Databricks cited a $7 billion revenue run rate and over 80% year-over-year growth; Sixth Street Growth joined as a new investor.

Databricks, CNBC, Crunchbase News

Alibaba ships Qwen3.8-27B open-weight model

Alibaba's Tongyi Lab released Qwen3.8-27B on August 14, a 27.78B-parameter Apache-2.0 multimodal model with a 262K native context that runs on a single ~16GB consumer GPU. It trended rapidly on Hugging Face, with reported downloads passing one million within a day.

AI Release Tracker, Kingy AI, Kie.ai

🐗 Arkansas

Pulaski County advances data-center regulations

The Pulaski County Quorum Court advanced an ordinance to regulate large-scale data centers, classifying them as heavy industrial uses requiring a Conditional Use Permit and sending it to the full body with a vote expected around August 25. The push followed a failed moratorium and centers on a proposed $1 billion Google data center and an AVAIO project. The county Planning Board postponed its recommendation on August 13.

Arkansas Democrat-Gazette, Arkansas Times, KARK, KATV

League of Women Voters asks Sanders to recall Guard troops

On August 11 the League of Women Voters of Arkansas asked Gov. Sarah Huckabee Sanders to recall the state's National Guard members from Washington, DC — a local tie-in to the national deployment story.

Arkansas Democrat-Gazette

Arkansas State welcomes its first vet-med class

Arkansas State University's new College of Veterinary Medicine welcomed its inaugural class of students on August 11. Separately, a federal judicial nomination advanced that could make its nominee the third Arkansan confirmed to the federal bench in 2026.

Arkansas Democrat-Gazette, Arkansas Times

📚 This Week in Arkansas History

Around this week in 1908, the Improved Order of Red Men — a fraternal order founded in 1834 — operated a sanitarium at 637 Park Avenue in Hot Springs, part of the spa city's turn-of-the-century boom in health resorts drawing visitors to its thermal waters.

🎬 Pop Culture

Phoebe Bridgers releases Lost Weekend

Phoebe Bridgers released her third solo album on August 14, her first in about six years since 2020's Punisher, via Dead Oceans. Shaped by the death of her father, the record drew strong reviews and features contributors including Jack Antonoff, Alex G and Bo Burnham.

Dead Oceans, NPR, Variety, Consequence, The Guardian

Spider-Man: Brand New Day holds No. 1 for a third weekend

The Sony release stayed atop the box office with about $70 million — the second-best third weekend ever behind The Force Awakens — and crossed $2.022 billion worldwide to become Sony's biggest title ever. Summer 2026 domestic box office topped $4.24 billion, the biggest post-COVID summer.

The Hollywood Reporter, Deadline, Variety

Actress Hayden Panettiere dies at 36

The Heroes and Nashville actress died August 16 in Greenville, South Carolina, at age 36. The cause and manner were pending an autopsy, with most reporting following the next day.

ABC News, NBC News
