The week's biggest US story was economic: the Federal Reserve held rates at 3.5%–3.75% in a rare 9–3 split on July 29, one day before the BEA's advance estimate showed Q2 GDP growth slowing to 1.5% while PCE inflation jumped to 5.1% annualized — a stagflation-tinged picture presided over by a divided Fed under new Chair Kevin Warsh. Politically, the week was dominated by fights over voting and the DOJ, with the administration filing an emergency Supreme Court appeal to enforce its mail-in-voting order and pressing the Senate to cancel its August recess. Abroad, President Trump announced a Gaza disarmament breakthrough while climate-driven wildfires forced hundreds of thousands of evacuations across France and Spain.

🏛️ US Politics & Policy

Federal Reserve holds rates in divided 9–3 vote; Q2 GDP slows to 1.5% as inflation heats up

The FOMC kept the federal funds rate at 3.5%–3.75% for a fifth straight meeting on July 29, but three regional presidents dissented in favor of a hike. One day later, the BEA's advance estimate showed real GDP grew at a 1.5% annualized rate in Q2 — below the ~2.0%–2.1% economists expected — while the PCE price index rose 5.1% annualized (core 3.4%). Real consumer spending actually accelerated to 3.2%, but a widening trade deficit and falling government spending dragged on the headline, leaving new Chair Kevin Warsh's Fed openly split.

Federal Reserve, Bureau of Economic Analysis, CNBC, CNN, EY

Trump administration asks Supreme Court to revive mail-in-voting order

On July 27, the DOJ filed an emergency application asking the justices to lift lower-court blocks on Trump's March executive order, which would deliver mail ballots only to people on a new "state citizenship list." A Boston-based 1st Circuit panel had upheld the block, finding the order would "sow confusion" and threaten disenfranchisement. Twenty-three Democratic-led states and DC challenged the order; the Court ordered them to respond by Aug. 3.

CNN, Washington Post, PBS NewsHour/AP, SCOTUSblog, Bloomberg

Senate Judiciary Committee stalls Todd Blanche's AG confirmation

On July 29, Chairman Chuck Grassley postponed the committee vote after GOP Sens. John Cornyn and Thom Tillis withheld support over a DOJ settlement Blanche oversaw that ended Trump's IRS lawsuit. The deal created a ~$1.8 billion "anti-weaponization" fund and granted Trump immunity from audits of past tax returns. Trump said July 30 he had "no objection to temporarily withdrawing Todd's name"; Blanche formally rescinded the fund on Aug. 3 to advance his confirmation.

NPR, CNBC, NBC News, Time, MS NOW

Trump demands Senate cancel August recess for the SAVE America Act

On July 27, Trump pressed Majority Leader John Thune to keep the chamber in session until it passes the SAVE America Act, a proof-of-citizenship voter registration bill, even urging elimination of the filibuster. With only a 53–47 majority, Thune said the votes aren't there, asking reporters "What's the picture of victory at the end?" The Brennan Center estimates more than 21 million voting-age citizens lack ready access to proof-of-citizenship documents.

PBS NewsHour/AP, The Hill, Spectrum News, News From The States

US-Iran conflict paused amid munitions concerns

The US halted nearly two weeks of strikes on Iran over the weekend of July 25–26, with Trump framing the pause as space for diplomacy while warning strikes could resume. CNN reported VP JD Vance and Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. Dan Caine had raised concerns about the US munitions stockpile. Oil prices tumbled sharply, with Brent down roughly 8.7% on July 27, while Iran's Foreign Ministry said no active negotiations were underway.

CNN, NBC News, Bloomberg, Axios, NPR, Al Jazeera

🌍 World News

Trump announces Gaza disarmament breakthrough, with major caveats

Late on July 31, Trump announced that Hamas had agreed to disarm in stages, with Israel to withdraw over time, after mediators from Egypt, Qatar, Turkey and the US finalized a roadmap. Hamas said implementation is conditional on Israeli withdrawal to the "Yellow Line," a halt to targeted killings, and a humanitarian surge. PM Netanyahu remained publicly silent, and Israel had not publicly agreed to the latest proposal.

CNN, Al Jazeera, NPR

Catastrophic wildfires force mass evacuations across France and Spain

Climate-driven wildfires forced enormous evacuations in late July, with CNN reporting more than 167,000 people evacuated in France's Gironde region and tens of thousands more near Madrid. President Macron called it "the most severe we have ever recorded, the toughest since the Second World War." Spain deployed thousands of personnel aided by crews from Greece, Italy and Portugal, and at least one person died as renewed heat threatened firefighters' fragile progress.

CNN, NPR, Al Jazeera

Middle East knock-on effects keep markets on edge

Beyond the strikes pause, Yemen's Houthis struck Saudi oil sites and traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remained disrupted. The combination kept global energy markets volatile even as oil prices fell on ceasefire hopes.

CNN, NBC News

💻 Tech News

Anthropic ships Claude Opus 5

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 on July 24, describing it as coming close to frontier intelligence "at half the price." Pricing held at $5/$25 per million input/output tokens, matching Opus 4.8, and it added a low/medium/high "effort" toggle to balance cost and capability. It was Anthropic's fourth Claude 5-tier release in under two months, underscoring a shift toward rapid, cost-focused iteration.

Anthropic (via TechCrunch, Bloomberg, Axios), 9to5Mac

A crowded July model wave

The week saw rapid releases across labs, including Alibaba's Qwen3.7 Flash on July 27 and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 on July 31. These followed OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family and xAI's Grok 4.5 earlier in the month.

LLM Gateway, AI Release Tracker, ThursdAI

PyTorch 2.13 released

The PyTorch Foundation shipped PyTorch 2.13 in July 2026, drawing 3,328 commits from 526 contributors. Updates spanned attention, compilation, distributed training, memory efficiency, and accelerator-platform support.

🐗 Arkansas

Gov. Sanders appoints Gary Stark to State Board of Education

On July 27, Governor Sarah Huckabee Sanders appointed Dr. Gary Stark to the nine-member Arkansas State Board of Education. Stark, a Cleburne County native, educator, and military veteran, replaces outgoing board chair Adrienne Woods; his term runs to June 30, 2033.

📚 This Week in Arkansas History

Little Rock Air Force Base, near Jacksonville in Pulaski County, was activated by Strategic Air Command on August 1, 1955, on roughly 6,100 acres donated by local landowners. It later hosted Titan II intercontinental ballistic missile operations in the 1960s and today is the primary US Air Force C-130 Hercules training base. (Sources agree on the SAC activation date, though the Encyclopedia of Arkansas's dedicated entry cites an Oct. 9, 1955 "official activation" open house.)

🎬 Pop Culture

"Spider-Man: Brand New Day" shatters box-office records

Sony/Marvel's "Spider-Man: Brand New Day" opened July 31 to roughly $355 million domestically — the second-biggest domestic opening ever, just behind "Avengers: Endgame." The film added $927M worldwide, earned an A CinemaScore and a 90% Rotten Tomatoes critics score, and set a $72M preview record. The weekend helped drive a record ~$429M North American frame.

Hollywood Reporter, CNN, Variety, Deadline, CNBC

Christopher Nolan's "The Odyssey" nears $1 billion

Universal's "The Odyssey" continued a strong run, reaching ~$395M domestically by the Aug. 1–2 weekend. The film helped push Universal past $4B in global year-to-date box office.

Hollywood Reporter, CNN, Deadline

Paramount pauses Warner Bros. megamerger amid antitrust suits

On July 24, Paramount Skydance agreed to postpone its ~$110B merger with Warner Bros. Discovery until five days after an antitrust trial or June 2027, whichever is earlier, after a federal judge issued a temporary restraining order in a suit by 12 states. The deal would fold in CNN and HBO Max, and Paramount faces a $7M-a-day "ticking fee" to WBD shareholders if it doesn't close by Sept. 30.

Washington Post, Variety, Axios, WBUR
