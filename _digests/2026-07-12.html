A foreign war and a domestic consolidation of power ran in parallel this week, each feeding the other. US strikes on Iran escalated into a multi-round naval conflict over the Strait of Hormuz, drawing five Gulf states into the crossfire and pushing oil higher, while at home the Trump administration subpoenaed four reporters, fired the entire bipartisan election-standards agency, and watched the Senate destabilize with the sudden death of Lindsey Graham and Mitch McConnell's disclosed illness. In tech, the AI model race hit a frenetic pace and developer tooling saw two of its biggest shifts in years.

🏛️ US Politics & Policy

Sen. Lindsey Graham dies suddenly at 71

Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC), a close Trump ally and the Senate's foremost defense hawk, died the evening of Saturday, July 11 of what his office called "a brief and sudden illness." The DC medical examiner's preliminary finding was aortic dissection due to arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease. Graham, 71, had just returned from Kyiv, where he met President Zelenskyy the day before, and was running for a fifth term; Gov. Henry McMaster will appoint a replacement, narrowing an already-thin GOP floor margin.

Axios, NBC News, The Hill

DOJ subpoenas four New York Times reporters over Air Force One coverage

The Justice Department subpoenaed Julian Barnes, Eric Lipton, Tyler Pager and Eric Schmitt after their reporting that the Secret Service urged Trump to avoid the new Qatari-gifted Air Force One over missing anti-missile countermeasures. Federal agents delivered some subpoenas to reporters' homes July 10, seeking grand jury testimony in Manhattan. The DOJ said "reporters are not the targets," while the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press said the move breaks from decades of practice of treating journalists as a last resort.

NPR, PBS NewsHour, Fox News

US launches repeated strikes on Iran as Hormuz standoff intensifies

US Central Command conducted several rounds of strikes, culminating in roughly 140 targets overnight July 11–12 and another wave Sunday, after IRGC forces attacked commercial shipping including the Cyprus-flagged GFS Galaxy. Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz closed; Trump insisted it remained open and floated charging other nations for passage. Sen. Adam Schiff said he would force another Senate vote to end the Iran war.

CNN, The Hill, CBS News

Trump fires remaining Election Assistance Commission members

Trump removed all three remaining members of the bipartisan Election Assistance Commission months before the midterms, leaving the agency — created by the 2002 Help America Vote Act to certify voting systems — without a quorum to set policy. The move followed the Supreme Court's late-June Trump v. Slaughter ruling expanding removal power over independent agencies. Democrats called it an attempt to dismantle an independent election guardrail.

Votebeat, NPR, Washington Post

Graham Platner ends Maine Senate bid after rape allegation

Democratic Senate candidate Graham Platner withdrew July 10 after a Maine resident alleged he raped her in late 2021, an accusation he denies. Platner, who had won the June primary with a record turnout, lost the backing of Bernie Sanders, Elizabeth Warren and the state party. Maine Democrats must pick a replacement to challenge GOP Sen. Susan Collins by July 27, a seat central to the party's hopes of flipping the chamber.

NPR, Washington Post, CBS News

McConnell discloses hospitalization, will not return to floor yet

Sen. Mitch McConnell (R-KY), 84, broke weeks of silence July 12 to disclose that a mid-June fall left him briefly unconscious and hospitalized, followed by pneumonia. He said doctors have advised he cannot yet return to the Senate floor to vote, though he continues legislative work from a rehabilitation center. He has not voted since June 11 and retires in January 2027.

The Hill, CNBC

🌍 World News

US–Iran conflict spreads across the Gulf

Iran answered US strikes with missile and drone attacks on five American allies — Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Jordan and the UAE, plus mediator Oman. Kuwait reported damage to an offshore oil rig, Qatar reported injuries including a child, and Bahrain urged residents to shelter. Oman drafted a tentative proposal to manage strait traffic as Iranian FM Araghchi met his Omani counterpart; CENTCOM said no US personnel were hurt.

CNN, CBS News, Al Jazeera

Ukraine's PM resigns in major government reshuffle

Prime Minister Yulia Svyrydenko resigned July 12 as President Zelenskyy announced a sweeping government overhaul, saying Ukraine "is changing its political strategy." The reshuffle — the fourth major reorganization since the full-scale invasion — came days after a Russian strike on Kyiv killed six, including a child, and as Ukraine pressed deep strikes on Russian oil refineries.

NBC News, CBS News, PBS NewsHour

UN warns Sudan's El Obeid could be the "next crime scene"

The UN Human Rights Council adopted a resolution July 6 establishing an urgent inquiry into the RSF siege of El Obeid, where roughly 500,000 civilians are trapped in North Kordofan. Investigators warned the siege echoes El Fasher, where more than 6,000 died in three days when the city fell in October 2025. Ten days of drone strikes killed at least 50 civilians.

UN News, Al Jazeera, Human Rights Watch

Hamas dissolves its Gaza governing body

Hamas announced July 6 it would dissolve the Emergency Committee that governed Gaza for nearly two decades, ceding civilian control to a technocratic body under UN Resolution 2803 and Trump's peace plan. It did not commit to disarmament, a key Israeli and US demand, and Israeli FM Gideon Saar dismissed the move as a "trick."

Al Jazeera, Time, Times of Israel

💻 Tech News

OpenAI ships GPT-5.6 and launches ChatGPT Work

OpenAI moved its GPT-5.6 family to general availability July 9 in three tiers — Sol, Terra and Luna — after a government safety review delayed the preview. Alongside it, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Work, an agent that gathers context across connected apps to produce documents, spreadsheets, slides and web apps, and merged the Codex app into a new ChatGPT desktop app.

Axios, TechCrunch, MacRumors

xAI launches Grok 4.5

xAI released Grok 4.5 on July 8, its first model since going public and moving to acquire coding startup Cursor. Trained alongside Cursor for agentic coding and priced at $2/$6 per million tokens, Musk called it "an Opus-class model, but faster." Independent benchmarker Artificial Analysis ranked it fourth on its Intelligence Index and found it notably token-efficient, though EU access remained pending.

xAI, Axios, TechCrunch

TypeScript 7 ships as a native Go port

Microsoft shipped TypeScript 7.0 on July 8, a native rewrite of the compiler in Go (Project Corsa) that Microsoft says is roughly 10x faster; a VS Code benchmark dropped a type-check from 125.7s to 10.6s. The type system is unchanged, but strict defaults are now hard-adopted and the stable programmatic API waits for 7.1, forcing tools like typescript-eslint to temporarily alias the prior release.

DevClass, Digital Applied

npm v12 disables install scripts by default

GitHub shipped npm v12, disabling dependency lifecycle scripts by default and blocking git and remote-tarball dependencies unless explicitly allowed — a major supply-chain hardening step after a year of registry attacks. A new strict setting turns skipped scripts into a hard error for CI, aligning npm with pnpm, Yarn and Bun.

The Hacker News, Socket

Microsoft cuts 4,800 jobs and spins off four Xbox studios

Microsoft cut about 4,800 jobs (2.1% of its workforce) July 6, with Xbox hit hardest. Double Fine and Compulsion Games returned to independence with their IP, while Ninja Theory and Undead Labs were sold to undisclosed owners. Xbox CEO Asha Sharma wrote that "our business today is not healthy," citing margins far below comparable platforms.

CNBC, TechCrunch, Kotaku

🐗 Arkansas

Arkansas named CNBC's "Most Improved State" for business

Arkansas jumped 13 spots to No. 28 in CNBC's 2026 America's Top States for Business — the largest improvement of any state — led by a 23-place leap to No. 13 for Workforce. The state added nearly 16,000 jobs in 2025, though CNBC still flagged bottom-half weaknesses in healthcare, education and infrastructure.

CNBC

Sanders touts federal education waiver

Gov. Sarah Huckabee Sanders joined US Education Secretary Linda McMahon in Hot Springs on July 7 to announce federal approval of Arkansas' "Returning Education to the States" waiver, framed as cutting bureaucracy and routing more dollars to classrooms. The state closed FY2026 with a $655 million surplus.

Governor's Office, Axios NW Arkansas

📚 This Week in Arkansas History

On July 8, 1897, the town of Marked Tree in Poinsett County was incorporated — per the Encyclopedia of Arkansas, possibly the only town by that name anywhere in the world.

🎬 Pop Culture

World Cup: USMNT out, Spain's clean-sheet record ends

All three co-hosts exited in the Round of 16, with the US men falling 4–1 to Belgium on July 6 in Seattle. In the July 10 quarterfinal, Spain goalkeeper Unai Simón's record 650-minute clean-sheet streak — the longest in World Cup history — ended on a Charles De Ketelaere header, though Spain still won 2–1 to reach the semifinals against France.

Yahoo Sports, CNN

David Attenborough becomes oldest Emmy nominee at 100

The 78th Primetime Emmy nominations, announced July 8, made Sir David Attenborough, 100, the oldest nominee in the awards' history with two Outstanding Narrator nods, surpassing Norman Lear. HBO Max's "The Pitt" led all programs with 25 nominations; the ceremony airs Sept. 14 on NBC.

Deadline, Variety
