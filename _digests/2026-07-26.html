The US–Iran war shaped nearly every corner of the week, driving dueling war-powers votes on Capitol Hill, sending oil past $100 a barrel, and pushing the Pentagon's post-February death toll to 18 US troops before both sides paused strikes on July 26. At home, the House sprinted through a party-line FY2027 budget and left town for a five-week recess, the administration replaced court-struck tariffs with a new forced-labor regime hitting 60 trading partners, and a federal appeals court kept Trump's voter-list order blocked. In tech, megacap earnings — not model launches — stole the week, as Alphabet's soaring capex guidance and Tesla's negative free cash flow reopened the question of whether record AI spending is paying off.

🏛️ US Politics & Policy

House passes second Iran war powers resolution; Senate companion fails

The House voted 214–208 on July 23 to pull US forces out of hostilities with Iran, with four Republicans — Tom Barrett, Warren Davidson, Thomas Massie, and Brian Fitzpatrick — joining every Democrat on the Jayapal-sponsored measure. It was the second time the chamber passed such a resolution, and it remains symbolic and non-binding. A companion joint resolution from Senate Democrats failed the same day, 47–49.

NPR, The Hill, CNBC

House adopts FY2027 budget resolution, then recesses until August 31

The House agreed to H.Con.Res.113 on a 216–214 party-line vote (all Republicans for, all Democrats against), establishing the FY2027 congressional budget. It also cleared the Stop Insider Trading Act (232–198) and the Removing Barriers to Work for Disabled Americans Act (232–188) before leaving for a district work period, while the Senate stayed in session.

Republican Cloakroom (house.gov), GovTrack

Trump reimposes tariffs on 60 trading partners under a forced-labor rationale

New Section 301 tariffs of 10% or 12.5% took effect at 12:01 a.m. July 24, hitting 60 partners that together account for roughly 99% of US imports — the higher rate for countries that hadn't adopted a forced-labor import ban. The levies replaced the Section 122 stopgap that expired the same day, itself a response to the Supreme Court striking down Trump's earlier emergency-powers tariffs. Trade analysts warned the new approach is likely to draw its own overreach challenge.

CBS News, Fortune, Washington Post, Time

Appeals court keeps Trump's federal voter-list order blocked

On July 25 the 1st US Circuit Court of Appeals upheld an injunction against Trump's March order to build a citizenship-based voter list and restrict mail-ballot delivery to people on it. The order was challenged by officials in 23 states and D.C.; the underlying injunction came from US District Judge Indira Talwani in Boston. The ruling keeps the order frozen in the suing states ahead of the November 3 midterms.

CBS News, Washington Post, Fortune

Jobless claims hit a 57-year low as the Fed heads into its meeting

Initial claims fell 22,000 to 187,000 for the week ending July 18 — the lowest since September 1969 — against forecasts near 210,000. The drop reinforced a "low hire, low fire" labor market following a June report of just 57,000 jobs added. The Fed, now chaired by Kevin Warsh, was set to meet July 28–29 with rates held at 3.50%–3.75% and markets expecting no change.

Bloomberg, Benzinga, WORLD

Executive order targets defense supply-chain vulnerabilities

Trump signed an order on July 20 requiring defense contractors to screen their supply chains for national-security risks, aiming to reduce reliance on foreign suppliers of concern, including China.

Washington Post

🌍 World News

US–Iran war escalates in the Strait of Hormuz, then pauses

Fighting ran for a 13th straight night before both sides paused strikes on July 26 as talks advanced. The Pentagon reported 18 US troops killed since fighting resumed in February, with four deaths since July 17 and nearly 500 injuries. Disruption to the strait — normally transited by about 110 ships a day — pushed global oil prices above $100 a barrel.

NPR, Encyclopedia Britannica, CNBC

US allies reject the new forced-labor tariffs

Australia, the EU, and New Zealand publicly criticized the tariffs on July 24. Australia's trade minister called the 12.5% rate "unjustified," while New Zealand's prime minister said the underlying findings were disputed and the move "extremely disappointing."

NPR

Indian activist ends 26-day hunger strike

Sonam Wangchuk ended a 26-day hunger strike that helped galvanize one of India's largest youth-led protest movements in recent years; demonstrations continued after police forcibly hospitalized him earlier in the week.

NPR

Assam flooding kills at least 50, displaces 700,000

Days of heavy monsoon rain overflowed the Brahmaputra River in India's northeast, killing at least 50 people and displacing 700,000. Officials said nearly 900 villages were submerged.

Morning Star

💻 Tech News

Megacap earnings reopen the AI-capex debate

Alphabet beat on revenue but fell more than 6% after hours when it raised 2026 capital-expenditure guidance to as high as $205 billion, well above expectations near $186 billion, even as Google Cloud grew 82% to $24.77 billion. Tesla's revenue rose 26% to $28.24 billion but adjusted EPS missed (33 cents vs. 51 expected), GAAP operating income fell 57% to $398 million, and free cash flow turned negative by about $1.1 billion as capex surged 142%. The pair sharpened investor pressure for spending discipline, not just top-line beats.

CNBC, Quartz, Yahoo Finance

Google ships three new Gemini Flash models

On July 21 Google released Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite, and a security-tuned 3.5 Flash Cyber restricted to governments and trusted partners — the tier where most production enterprise volume runs. The flagship 3.5 Pro slipped again, and Google used the announcement to tease Gemini 4 pretraining.

9to5Google, Build Fast with AI

Apple readies a lease-to-own hardware program

Apple is reportedly preparing "Apple Upgrade," a lease-to-own plan letting US customers spread iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Apple Watch costs across multi-year payments financed by Klarna, with a launch expected July 28. Treat as reported rather than confirmed pending Apple's own announcement.

🐗 Arkansas

North Little Rock swears in its first Black police chief

Hayward Finks was sworn in July 23 as North Little Rock's first Black chief of police, pledging to uphold the department's reputation while reaching out to residents who are skeptical of or neutral toward the agency.

LEARNS Act school-choice spending draws fresh scrutiny

State Department of Education data showed just 10 private schools drew more than $60 million in taxpayer funds during the 2025–26 year through the LEARNS Act school-choice program, reviving debate over how the universal accounts are distributed.

Christopher Nolan's "The Odyssey" grossed an estimated $87 million in its second weekend (July 24–26), topping "The Dark Knight" for the best second weekend of his career and lifting its domestic total to $286.3 million and global cume to $639.6 million. No new wide release opened against it.
