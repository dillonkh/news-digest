The week was defined by a sudden return to direct US-Iran hostilities after a month-long lull, set against an unusually dense run of Trump-administration foreign-policy and economic moves — US majority control of Venezuela's oil, a Supreme Court win on mail-voting rules, and a grid-security emergency order. Overseas, catastrophic glacial floods on the Nepal–China border became the deadliest single event of the window, and Norway's King Harald V died at 89. In markets and tech, new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh used Jackson Hole to steer expectations toward tighter policy while Nvidia posted a record quarter.

🏛️ US Politics & Policy

US and Iran resume direct strikes over the Strait of Hormuz

US Central Command struck Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island over the weekend of August 30, asserting Iran was preparing to lay naval mines and posed an "imminent threat," killing and wounding several Iranian soldiers and civilians. Iran's Revolutionary Guard retaliated Monday with missiles and drones against US targets at the King Hussein and Al Azraq air bases in Jordan and a base in the UAE; Jordan said its air defenses destroyed eight missiles that crossed its airspace. It was the first kinetic exchange since July, ending a lull in which the administration had leaned on secondary sanctions and a naval blockade.

CNN, The Hill, Washington Times, New Arab

Trump announces US majority stake in Venezuela's oil reserves

On August 28, Trump announced an agreement giving the US an effective 55% output stake in Venezuela's more than 65 billion barrels of proven reserves, negotiated by Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and acting Venezuelan president Delcy Rodríguez. A US official said a newly created private company received 100-year development rights "at no cost" to taxpayers, making it the second-largest corporate holder of proven reserves after Saudi Aramco. The deal follows the January 2026 US operation that captured then-president Nicolás Maduro; terms came partly from an anonymous official and implementation had not yet begun.

NPR, CNBC, NBC News

Supreme Court lets part of Trump's mail-voting order proceed

In Trump v. California (No. 26A124), the Court on August 24 granted a stay allowing part of Trump's executive order restricting mail ballots for the 2026 midterms to take effect, lifting Judge Indira Talwani's injunction. The majority acted on narrow procedural grounds — that the challenge was premature because no final rule had issued when the complaint was filed — and did not defend the order's underlying lawfulness. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson dissented, noting that no judge or justice had held the order comports with the Constitution, and a parallel challenge remains pending in federal court in Washington, D.C.

Votebeat, Supreme Court of the United States

Fed Chair Warsh signals inflation focus at Jackson Hole

In his first Jackson Hole keynote as chair on August 28, Kevin Warsh said the central bank still has "work to do" on inflation, making price stability the top priority and declining explicit forward guidance. Markets read it hawkishly: the policy-sensitive 2-year Treasury yield rose more than 12 basis points to 4.356%. Outlet projections of a specific rate-hike path are analyst forecasts, not FOMC commitments — the committee had not met.

Federal Reserve, CNBC, Fortune, Forbes

Trump declares grid-security emergency

On August 26, Trump declared a national emergency and directed the Department of Energy to draft rules limiting foreign-made equipment — transformers, batteries, inverters, and associated software — that utilities can connect to the bulk-power system. The move fits the administration's broader energy-security posture, though specifics rested on a single outlet at the time of writing.

🌍 World News

Glacial collapse triggers catastrophic Nepal–China floods

A glacier collapse on Langtang Lirung on August 26 sent flash floods down a 72-km stretch of the Trishuli River, destroying the Gyirong border checkpoint and dozens of settlements. As of September 1, Nepal counted 987 dead and 3,916 missing — including 583 foreign nationals across 38 countries — with 11,814 rescued; China reported 16 dead and 546 missing in Tibet. Newly formed barrier lakes upstream kept evacuation warnings in effect, and casualty figures were still rising at publication.

CNN, Wikipedia, NPR

Norway's King Harald V dies at 89

Europe's oldest reigning monarch died August 28 at Oslo University Hospital of complications from haemolytic anaemia, after 10 days hospitalized; the Royal Palace said he "passed away peacefully." His son Crown Prince Haakon automatically succeeded him as King Haakon VIII, with Mette-Marit as queen and granddaughter Ingrid Alexandra now heir. Harald, who fled the Nazi occupation to the US as a child, reigned for 35 years and lay in state at the palace chapel from August 31.

Euronews, AP, CNN, NPR, Washington Post

DOJ seizes Chinese hacking infrastructure

Late in the week the Justice Department seized control of websites for two hacking platforms, "QScan" and "QTRouter," tied to a nearly decade-long campaign the FBI said targeted US government agencies, utilities, telecoms, and hospital systems by routing attacks through a botnet of compromised home routers and smart devices. Specific victim claims were FBI-attributed and rested on a single outlet at the time of writing.

💻 Tech News

Nvidia posts a record quarter

Nvidia reported fiscal Q2 revenue of $96.2 billion, up 106% year over year, beating the roughly $92.2B consensus, with data-center revenue of $89.0 billion (up 117%) and adjusted EPS of $2.22. It guided Q3 to about $108.0 billion — above consensus — while assuming essentially zero China data-center revenue. Shares slipped modestly after hours as the company guided gross margin down toward 71–72% by fiscal Q4 on rising memory costs.

CNBC, Yahoo Finance, Kiplinger

An August 27 report said Nvidia had agreed to acquire the open-source model hub Hugging Face for nearly $13 billion. The story was single-sourced and unconfirmed — neither party had commented publicly — and should be treated as an unverified report pending official or wire confirmation.

🐗 Arkansas

Little Rock adopts new high school attendance zones

After more than four hours of public comment on August 27, the Little Rock School Board voted 5–2 to adopt "Option 3.5," redrawing high school zones to accommodate the new Little Rock West High School opening in fall 2027. The board also unanimously adopted a resolution letting students remain at their current schools or choose one regardless of the new zones. District enrollment has fallen 21.2% since late 2013, from 23,676 to 18,648.

Arkansas Democrat-Gazette, KATV, Arkansas Times, KARK

Statewide survey finds 37% of Arkansans food-insecure

Research from the Arkansas Hunger Relief Alliance and the state's six Feeding America food banks, released August 26, found that 37% of Arkansans — more than 1.14 million people — don't have enough food to meet their needs, versus 27% nationally. The study drew on more than 1,700 phone interviews and about 3,563 paper surveys, and organizers noted charitable food distribution has nearly doubled over a decade even as insecurity rose.

Arkansas Democrat-Gazette, KARK, Arkansas Times, Talk Business & Politics

2.29-carat yellow diamond registered at Crater of Diamonds

The state park near Murfreesboro announced that Barry G. Smith of Germantown, Tennessee, had registered a 2.29-carat canary-yellow diamond — the "Sage Juniper Diamond" — found in the East Drain, the third-largest registered at the park in 2026. The stone was found August 16; the public announcement fell within this window, and corroboration leans on regional outlets.

WMC Action News 5, AY Magazine

📚 This Week in Arkansas History

On August 26, 1958, Governor Orval Faubus convened an extraordinary session of the Arkansas General Assembly, which passed sixteen bills including Act 4, authorizing the closure of any school threatened with integration. Faubus used it to shut all four Little Rock public high schools for 1958–59 — the "Lost Year" — until federal courts struck the laws down on June 18, 1959.

🎬 Pop Culture

"Spider-Man: Brand New Day" holds No. 1

The Sony/Marvel film held the top box-office spot for a fifth weekend on August 28 with roughly $18M (-54%). It had opened August 1–3 to a $360 million domestic debut — the biggest opening weekend in box-office history, edging out Avengers: Endgame's $357M — on a global bow near $932M.

Deadline, NBC News, Variety

US Open main draws begin

The year's final Grand Slam opened its 128-player men's and women's singles main draws on August 30 at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing Meadows, with defending champions Carlos Alcaraz and Aryna Sabalenka headlining. Finals are set for September 12–13.
